- [Safety and efficacy of feed additives consisting of tinctures derived from the roots or leaves and stems of Althaea officinalis L.: Marsh mallow root tincture for use in all animal species and marsh mallow leaf tincture for use in horses and dogs (FEFANA…](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2026.10346)
  - Fuente: EFSA · Tipo: opinion_cientifica · Fecha: 2026-10-09 · Score: 19
  - Extracto oficial: Following a request from the European Commission, EFSA was asked to deliver a scientific opinion on the safety and efficacy of two tinctures obtained from the roots (marsh mallow root tincture) or leaves and stems (marsh mallow leaf tincture) of Althaea officinalis L. when used as sensory additives. The additives are water/ethanol tinctures, with a specified dry matter content of 2%–3% (w/w) (marsh mallow root tincture) and not more than 25% (w/w) (marsh mallow leaf tincture). Marsh mallow root tincture is intended for use in feed or water for drinking for all animal species, while marsh mallow leaf tincture is proposed for use in feed for horses and dogs. The EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP) concluded that marsh mallow root tincture is safe at the maximum proposed use level of 250 mg/kg complete feed for all animal species. Marsh mallow leaf tincture is safe at the maximum proposed use level of 1000 mg/kg complete feed for horses and 325 mg/kg complete feed for dogs. No safety concern would arise for the consumer and the environment from the use of marsh mallow tinctures in animal feed. The additives under assessment should be considered as irritant to skin and eyes, and as dermal and respiratory sensitisers. Exposure of users by any route is considered a risk. Since the roots of A. officinalis are recognised to flavour food and their function in feed would be essentially the same as that in food, no further demonstration of efficacy for marsh mallow root tincture was considered necessary. The same conclusions are extended to marsh mallow leaf tincture.

The additives are already authorised for use in chickens for fattening and reared for laying and breeding, weaned piglets and pigs for fattening. The FEEDAP Panel concludes that the additives remain safe for the target species, consumers and environment and that the extension of the use to suckling piglets would not affect these conclusions. There is no need for assessing the efficacy of the additives in the context of the renewal of the authorisation for chickens for fattening, chickens reared for laying and breeding, weaned piglets and pigs for fattening. The Panel concludes that the additives are efficacious in feed for suckling piglets at 600 mg/kg complete feed.
- [Scoping review of existing in silico and PBK models for humans and rats](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/sp.efsa.2026.EN-10362)
  - Fuente: EFSA · Tipo: opinion_cientifica · Fecha: 2026-10-06 · Score: 8
  - Extracto oficial: One of EFSA research priority research areas is to better understand the role of toxicokinetic and its implementation into next generation risk assessment. Food ingredients and contaminants often lack data on their absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion (ADME) properties, which are needed to model their bioavailability using Physiologically Based Kinetic (PBK) models. The project ADME4NGRA, “Implementing the EFSA NAMs roadmap through advancing toxicokinetic knowledge in chemical risk assessment”, aims to provide a list of NAM‐based ADME tools and guidance for parameterising PBK models for rats and humans. The first step (and aim of this report) is to characterise the landscape of available in silico models, in vitro‐in vivo extrapolation (IVIVE) methodologies and PBK models. The results of a scoping review and the assessment of currently available generic PBK platforms as well as newly developed and existing in silico models are herein reported and discussed, highlighting knowledge and data gaps relevant for the assessment of compounds occurring in food and feed.
